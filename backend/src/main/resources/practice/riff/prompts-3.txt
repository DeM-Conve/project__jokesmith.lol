- I asked to remove it and the whole kitchen went silent like a Western.
- I tipped on top because I panicked. The waiter is now my financial advisor.
- It's 12.5%. That's not a number, that's a tax with a smile.

> The restaurant's menu has no prices.
- Which is how you know it costs about the same as a used car.
- I ordered the cheapest-sounding thing. "Bread." It was £18.
- I've never been more nervous in my life. I ordered water like it was a big decision.
- When the bill arrived they placed it face down. That's how they hand out grim diagnoses.

> The waiter asked "Is everything all right?" while I had a full mouth.
- I gave him a thumbs up and a sort of gargled moan like a happy walrus.
- They're trained to do it. It's a power move. It's the restaurant version of the dentist.
- I said "mmmhmm" with such conviction he brought me another one.
- I nodded so hard I swallowed an olive whole. Everything is not all right now.

> The gym has a sign that says "Please wipe down equipment after use."
- I wipe down the equipment before use. I'm not sure who's been on that rowing machine. Possibly a horse.
- I use so many wipes I'm basically a cleaner with a gym membership.
- Some people treat a bench like a sponge. Others treat it like a crime scene.
- I wiped it down so hard it now shines more than my career.

> There's a man at the gym who grunts on every rep.
- It's less a workout and more a nature documentary about walruses mating.
- I've started grunting back. We're communicating now. I think I owe him money.
- His grunts have different meanings. One means "set done." One means "I'm in pain." One means "Brenda."
- He's lifting 10 kilos. The grunt weighs more than the weight.

> There's a queue for the queue.
- It's like the Russian doll of British culture.
- I joined the wrong one. I'm now waiting for something I didn't want, very politely.
- I asked someone what the queue was for. He didn't know. He was just enjoying it.
- I'm British. If I see a queue, I join it. That's how I ended up with a flu jab and a Gregg's.

> Someone pushed in front of me in the queue.
- I said nothing, but I did tut. A tut so precise it could cut glass.
- In my head I've already written to the council, my MP and his mother.
- I'm going to think about this every night for eleven years and once at my wedding.
- He bought one stamp. One stamp! You don't push in for a stamp. You push in for a kidney.

> I got seated next to the kids' table at a wedding.
- By the speeches I'd been accepted into their gang and taught a handshake I'm sworn never to reveal.
- They were the most intelligent conversation I'd had all year. One of them explained crypto better than my bank.
- Their table had a colouring book and crayons. Mine had a single breadstick and a cousin named Darren.
- The best man made a joke and a seven-year-old said "that's not funny" with the confidence of a critic.

> The wedding invite says "adults only, no plus ones."
- Which is a polite way of saying "you're single, we know, we've planned accordingly."
- I RSVP'd with an exact head count: "Just me, and my enormous sense of humour about this."
- I'm bringing my dignity. Apparently that also doesn't count.
- I'm seated on the "misfit table" with a dentist, a lone uncle and someone's ex-roommate from 2006.

> The DJ at the wedding just played "Come On Eileen."
- Within eight seconds, every uncle in the room took his tie off and turned it into a headband.
- It's the national anthem of people who don't dance but have been drinking since noon.
- There's a speed-up bit in the middle that's caused more slipped discs than rugby.
- No one is called Eileen. But I've seen men cry to it like they've lost an Eileen.

> The cinema charged £9 for popcorn.
- For that price I want each kernel to have been raised in a meadow.
- It's the only time I've ever been mugged and handed a bucket.
- It's 90% air, 10% salt and 100% regret by the time the trailers finish.
- I had to take out a loan. They give you the size "large" so you feel like you got something for your mortgage.

> Someone's phone went off during the play.
- Their ringtone was the Crazy Frog. The actor on stage was performing King Lear. Shakespeare would have loved it.
- Three hundred people turned around at the same time like a flock of disappointed owls.
- They answered it. And said "I'm at the theatre." Loudly. Like we didn't know.
- The actor paused, waited for silence, then delivered the line "Who's there?" Best moment of the night.

> The hairdresser asked "Going anywhere nice on holiday?"
- I said no. She looked so disappointed I made up a trip to Croatia. Now she wants photos.
- I'm going nowhere. I'm here, in a chair, staring at a version of myself with wet hair and doubts.
- It's December. My holiday was eight months ago and it was to my mum's.
- I panicked and said "Mars." We spent forty minutes discussing the weather on Mars.

> The man at the barbecue is "just going to check the meat."
- He's checked it 37 times. He's not cooking. He's in a relationship with it.
- He's standing over it with tongs like a man defusing a bomb.
- Every time he lifts the lid the sausages get a bit more spiritual.
- He keeps saying "two more minutes." It's been two more minutes for an hour.

> At the pub quiz, our team name was too clever and nobody got it.
- It was a pun on a 1970s Belgian politician. The quizmaster read it out like a hostage.
- We came last but our team name got a round of applause from one bloke in the corner.
- Our previous team name was "Quiz Team Aguilera." That got a laugh. This one got a ban.
- We came second last, beaten by a team called "Dave."

> The sign at the swimming pool says "No petting."
- I'm not sure who they think is coming here for a romantic evening between the verruca socks.
- Next to it, another sign says "No bombing." Honestly, my swimming pool's like a war zone.
- They've also banned running, diving and fun. It's basically a Victorian church with chlorine.
- The lifeguard is sixteen and enforces it like he's guarding the crown jewels.

> The man at the pub said "I'm not being funny, but."
- Then he wasn't. He was absolutely right about that part.
- In my experience those words are followed by either racism or a strong opinion about roundabouts.
- It's the "I'm not a doctor, but" of pub conversation. Always ends in a diagnosis of everyone.
- He was being funny. Accidentally. He said Belgium was a myth.

> It's 30 degrees and the British public has lost its mind.
- A man in my street is mowing his lawn shirtless. He's seventy. It's 7am. It's not a lawn; it's a statement.
- Everyone's out in the park, lying on towels, turning the colour of a prawn cocktail.
- There's a hosepipe ban and my neighbour's bought a paddling pool that looks like a lake.
- By lunchtime the trains were cancelled because the tracks had "become too hot." We don't know what to do with weather.

> The forecast says "feels like minus 4."
- Feels like to whom? It's minus 4 for the penguin and a light breeze for my dad, who's out in a T-shirt.
- Weather's the only thing allowed to say "it's not really this bad, but emotionally it is."
- My car door is frozen shut and I'm pouring a kettle on it like I'm baptising a Vauxhall.
- I've got so many layers on that if I fall over I'll just have to live there now.

> The first snow of the year just fell.
- Half a centimetre. The trains have stopped, the schools have shut and Waitrose has run out of oat milk.
- Every child on the street built a snowman. It's about four inches tall and 70% gravel.
- My neighbour has gritted his drive like he's expecting a convoy. He doesn't own a car.
- By noon it was grey slush with a carrot in it, like a crime scene at a vegetable stall.

> I wore shorts and it immediately started raining.
- I didn't dress for the weather. I dressed for my optimism.
- Shorts are the most powerful rain spell in Britain. Cheaper than a rain dance.
- My knees have never been so humiliated. They're shivering in public.
- I looked like a boy scout who'd fallen in a pond.

> It's August and the shops have started selling Christmas stuff.
- There's a Santa on the shelf next to the sun cream, sweating in solidarity.
- By the time Christmas actually arrives those mince pies will be old enough to vote.
- I've bought an advent calendar and eaten it already. I'm fasting until December now.
- The shop assistant wished me a merry Christmas and I was wearing flip-flops. We both felt awful.

> Autumn's here and every street is just wet leaves.
- Walking to work now is like crossing an ice rink made of soggy cornflakes.
- I slipped on one in front of a bus queue and did a little curtsy so it looked deliberate.
- The council leaf-blower man just moves them from one end of the street to the other. It's a job with no ending, like painting a bridge.
- Leaves on the line, leaves on the path, leaves in my shoes. Autumn is just the weather doing admin.

> I phoned the council about a missing bin.
- They asked me to describe it. "Green. Bin-shaped." That's every bin, Linda.
- I was transferred to Waste, then Highways, then a man called Ray who I think was just a man called Ray.
- They said they'd send a new one in six to eight weeks. I'll be throwing rubbish at the moon by then.
- Turns out it was in my neighbour's drive. He'd numbered it. He'd painted the number out. He'd committed to this.

> The form says "Please write in block capitals."
- I'm not a builder. I write in the same handwriting I learned when I was five and sad.
- I panicked and started shouting at the paper.
- My capitals look like my signature, which looks like I fell down the stairs with a pen.
- The box is three millimetres wide and asks for my full name, including middle ones I've buried.

> I need to prove my address but every bill is online.
- They want a paper letter. The last paper letter I got was a Valentine's in Year 8.
- I offered them my Amazon history. They didn't take it, but they did judge me.
- The only paper with my address on it is a pizza menu. It's not legally binding.
- I'm officially homeless in the eyes of the bank, but my Deliveroo driver knows exactly where I live.

> My passport photo makes me look like a hostage.
- Not smiling, neutral background, ears visible. It's a mugshot with paperwork.
- The border guard looked at it, looked at me and said "rough year?"
- I took it in a booth at Tesco. Six attempts. The final one looked like I'd seen a ghost through a hedge.
- It's valid for ten years. That's ten years of looking like I've just been told the Wi-Fi's down.

> The government website says the service is "unavailable outside office hours."
- It's a website. It doesn't have a commute. It isn't tired. It's just sulking with the lights on.
- Apparently the internet goes home at five and has a bath.
- I logged in at 5:01 and it showed me a picture of a closed door. Literally a door.
- It reopens at nine, which is when everyone who needs it is at work. Elegant.

> The letter from the tax office starts with "Dear Sir/Madam."
- Nothing good ever started with a slash.
- They don't know my gender but they know what I spent on a hot tub in 2019.
- I read the first line and then lay down on the hall carpet for a while.
- It's a brown envelope. Brown envelopes are just bad news wearing a cardigan.

> My parking permit expired yesterday.
- The traffic warden was waiting in the bushes like a lion that's read the regulations.
- I got a ticket at 12:01. He must have been in the car with me, counting.
- Renewal requires proof of address, proof of car, proof of me and a short essay on why I deserve it.
- I'm now parking three streets away and walking home like a fox.

> The terms and conditions are 48 pages long.
- I scrolled to the bottom so fast I agreed to give them my firstborn and a kidney.
- Somewhere on page 31 I've promised to never speak ill of a printer company.
- Nobody's ever read these. They could put a recipe in the middle and we'd never know.
- I clicked "I agree" with the same confidence I say "yes" to the dentist asking if I floss.

> The office says "take a ticket and wait to be called."
- I'm number 84. They're on 12. I've seen seasons change in this room.
- I've made friends here. We've formed a book club. Number 51 has proposed to number 60.
- My ticket says 84 and the display says "B07." There are two systems. Nobody explains the B.
- I fell asleep and missed my number. Now I'm 211. I'll see you in spring.

> I was told to "fill in the form online" and then print it, sign it and post it.
- That's not digital. That's paper with extra steps and a modem.
- The last time I owned stamps, they had the Queen on them looking twenty years younger.
- I don't own a printer, so I'm going to a library to print a form for a website that sent me there.
- I filled it in online, printed it, posted it, and they emailed me a reply asking me to phone.

> My kid's school sent home a letter about "World Book Day."
- I've got eleven hours to turn a Tesco bag into a costume of a character from a book I've never read.
- Last year he went as "a man reading." It was my dad's coat and a newspaper. He won.
- Every parent in the playground is wearing the face of someone who's just used a glue gun at 2am.
- Apparently he's going as "Hungry Caterpillar." I've made him a sleeping bag with eyes.

> My teacher said "I'm not angry, I'm just disappointed."
- That's worse. Angry passes. Disappointment lives in your bones until you're forty.
- I'm 34 and I still think about it every time I don't recycle properly.
- Then she said "you've let yourself down." I hadn't even been involved. I was just standing near Darren.
- I'd have preferred detention. Detention ends at four. Disappointment never clocks off.

> I'm taking an evening class in pottery.
- So far I've made two bowls, an ashtray and a thing my teacher describes as "brave."
- I wanted to feel like Patrick Swayze. Instead I feel like a man trying to strangle a wet pigeon.
- The class is full of divorced dads finding themselves in clay.
- My mug leans so much it's technically a ramp.

> I'm forty and I've just enrolled in a Spanish course.
- After six weeks I can order a beer, apologise, and tell someone the cat is under the table. Which covers most of my evenings.
- A nineteen-year-old in my class was fluent by week three. I learned "hola" and humility.
- I've got a 300-day Duolingo streak and I still can't ask for directions. But the owl is proud of me, and that's enough.
- I practised on a waiter in Málaga. He replied in perfect English, out of pity.

> The school sent a parents' evening slot at 4:17pm.
- 4:17. Not quarter past. Not twenty past. They're running it like a NASA launch.
- I've got four minutes to hear about my child's entire future. That's less time than I spend at a cashpoint.
- The maths teacher overran by six minutes and now the whole evening is like an airport with no planes.
- I sat in a chair built for an eight-year-old and got told my child "lacks focus." So does the furniture budget.

> My son's homework is so hard I had to google it.
- I'm a grown adult with a mortgage, and I've been beaten by a fraction about pizzas.
- They teach maths differently now. It involves "number bonds." I've only got savings bonds and they're worse.
- He got a gold star. I got a gold star. We split it. It's on the fridge with my self-esteem.
- I asked him what a "fronted adverbial" is. He looked at me like I'd asked what a tree is.

> I bumped into my old PE teacher.
- He looked at me and said "still can't climb a rope, then?" I'm forty-one. I have a pension.
- He's still wearing the exact same tracksuit. It's either a different tracksuit, or he's a ghost.
- I instinctively apologised for not bringing my kit.
- He's in his seventies now. He jogged off. I got the bus.
